# Belvedere — current design

**Spoilers:** this covers the puzzle solutions, hidden items and the ending.

This file holds the current design and the reasons behind non-obvious choices. Edit sections in place when the design changes; do not append history. Exact values live in code; numbers here are the ones Nick chose deliberately.

## Conventions
- Slot index = row*3 + col; row 0 = north, col 0 = west; slot 4 = center.
- Room id = home slot: 0 Bedroom, 1 Bathroom, 2 Study, 3 Kitchen, 5 Living, 6 Dining, 7 Foyer, 8 Laundry; -1 = empty slot (gap).
- Directions N=0 E=1 S=2 W=3. A room side is a doorway if it is interior in the solved layout, otherwise a window. Foyer south is the front door and blocks walking like a window.

## Board and rules
- 3×3 grid, eight rooms, one gap. Rooms never rotate. 6 m interiors, 0.25 m walls (two 0.125 m halves, one per room, so faces never coincide), 6.25 m slot pitch, 3 m ceilings, eye height 1.65 m, 3 m/s walking.
- Glazed double doors open automatically when the facing side is the gap or another room's doorway; otherwise they stay shut and both leaves rattle on use. Windows never allow walking, but slides are legal across any side.
- Lever: hold E or left mouse on it, push mouse/WASD (view-relative, converted to world axes), release. Into the gap = the whole room slides with the player riding; otherwise a wooden clunk. Slide = 1.6 s (0.09 s take-up, 1.43 s travel with half-second braking, 0.08 s docking), no overshoot or shake; input is locked and board state commits once on docking. Doors close in transit and re-evaluate on landing.
- The gap is dark concrete with flush crossing tracks, recessed 2 cm to avoid coplanar faces with passing rooms. Matching stationary slabs cover both the departing and destination slots throughout a shift, so newly exposed ground already has tracks before the room lands. When the gap is on the perimeter you can walk out onto the lawn and back in through it. Exterior doors stay blocked.
- Every room carries its furniture, fixtures, clues and floor patterns when it slides.

## Starts and modes
- Default launch = seed A opening `[6,2,5,0,1,3,7,8,-1]`: Laundry starts bottom-center, with the gap to its right. The game opens on the Bedroom wall title. Other seeds keep their original spawns, 1.7 m behind the lever facing the gap, and skip the wall title.
- `--seed=B|C|random|<index>` picks from `data/accepted_14_20.json`: 1,816 states that are 14–20 optimal slides from solved, with the player's room having a doorway open to the gap. The spec's random-walk scramble was replaced because simulation (`tools/sim/`) showed random walks barely leave solved. Only 7% of boards are reachable; there are no dead states.
- `--web-entry` exercises the web play-gesture path natively.

## Rooms and furnishings
Accent colours: Bedroom dusty blue, Bathroom sea-glass, Study deep green, Kitchen mustard, Living terracotta, Dining plum, Foyer cream/wood with a checker floor, Laundry slate blue. The accent goes on one wall and on the door casings, so a room can be identified through any neighbour's glass.
- Original low-poly furniture with simple colliders; only the reward drawers animate. Keep a 1.2 m ring clear around each lever and clear approaches to all four sides. Exceptions: Bedroom (the bed and 4 m-wide rug are centered between the two north windows, with the bedside table on the west side; the lever is approached from the east, south or west, and the north windows via the west aisle) and Dining (a 2.3 m table 1.55 m west of the lever, with an aisle around both ends). Tall storage stays on solid walls; window sightlines stay clear.
- Foyer: trophy cabinet NW, bench NE, coat and umbrella stands SE, console/mirror SW. Study safe under the east windows between the globe and reading chair. Bathroom: tub NW, a 1.5 m washstand centered on the SW south-wall section with its basin and mirror centered above, a 1.5 m bench beneath the NE window turned 12° toward the room with its towel rail on the east wall, and an ivory porcelain toilet with a wooden seat and brass flush handle centered on the east-wall section south of the doorway, facing west. The tub is pulled out of its NW corner and turned 8° into the room.
- Wall art: one small wood-framed, wordless print each in Bathroom (sea-glass waves on the SE south-wall section beside the toilet), Study (muted green botanical above the reading chair), Kitchen (ochre landscape between the west windows), Living (terracotta arch above the sofa), and Dining (plum vase above the sideboard). Original SVGs in `assets/wall_art/`, rendered as printed paper; no lights, animation, interaction, or collision. Bedroom, Foyer, and Laundry keep their existing wall pieces.
- House plan: a framed wordless floor plan (`assets/house_plan.svg`, from `tools/gen_plan.py`) hangs between the Laundry's east windows, in line with its west door, the lever and its pendant lamp. It teaches the core rule: every outer side has two windows, every inner side a double door, and the solid front door with its step is the only asymmetry. No room names, colours or numbers, so it gives the rule and not the answer. It is drawn facing the wall (east up, south right), so the Laundry is its top-right square. White paper and blue ink use the shared toon shader's `printed` mode. If it proves too small, the fallback is an inspect zoom.
- Decorative lamps stay unlit, so only the home lamps signal game state. Mirrors are painted highlights, not reflections.
- Budgets including the moving drawer and coin: ≤68 furniture/trim meshes in Bedroom and ≤90 in other rooms.

## Lamps, tokens, player piano
- The goal is the assembled house. Nothing points at one room to do next; a lit target pushed players after one room at a time.
- Lamps: each of the seven rooms with a coin drawer (all except Foyer) has a pendant that is warm white `#FFF0DA` while that room is home and off otherwise. The seven Foyer chandelier lights show the same thing.
- Seed A: the Laundry's slide east from bottom-center is the only slide reachable from the opening rooms. The first time a shift brings a lamp room home with the player aboard, a one-time guided look eases the camera to its lamp, holds, shows it turning warm, and returns control. It stops at the lamp and never shows a drawer.
- Seven physical coin drawers are integrated into existing furniture: Bedroom dresser, Study writing desk, Bathroom washstand, Kitchen counter beside the range, Living low sideboard, Dining sideboard, and Laundry folding table. A drawer's latch releases audibly 1.2 s after its room comes home, after the slide and door sounds. Trying it with E/click opens it only while its room is home; otherwise it rattles. Drawers never open by themselves. An opened drawer slides out over 0.65 s and stays open, even if its room later leaves home; an unopened one simply stops working again. The blue coin rests in the tray until directly picked up with E/click, which flies from that visible coin into the reward presentation. Empty drawers remain visibly open and can rattle without closing. Open drawers, waiting coins and collected state ride rooms through shifts and survive sleep/fall resets. `tools/capture.sh drawers A screenshots/drawers` checks all seven walking approaches, interactions, and open/empty views.
- Rewards (coins, binoculars, stopwatch, red key, trophy) fly to a presentation in front of the camera and stay until any fresh key, mouse button or gamepad button is pressed. That press is consumed. Presses made while the reward is still arriving are swallowed. Mouse motion and the wheel never accept. Each queued reward needs its own press. Presentations wait during pause, slides, recovery and item inspection. The large coin has a stamped bell emblem and appears on the left beside “Token” and “Good for one song”, using the same Fondamento typography and caption layout as binocular rewards. Captions hide while a presentation is suspended and fade as the item enters inventory. The HUD coin is unlettered and it and its count hide while the wallet is empty, including after spending; nothing glows.
- Player piano: an upright against the Living room's north wall, east of the door. Each fresh E/click spends one token, with a visible insertion and recorded clink, and plays the next new song; there is one per token. The sound comes from the piano and rides with the room, on the Music bus, and the main theme fades out for the song and back afterwards. Keys dip roughly in time and the roll turns only while a song plays, pausing with the menu. With no token in hand, pressing replays unlocked songs in turn for free, and does nothing while one is playing. With nothing unlocked, the mechanism only catches. Tokens buy nothing else: they are separate from the trophy, the bells and the finale.

## Bells and trophy
- Six plain wall bells spell the house's name, each on the inside of its room's accent wall: B Bedroom, E Dining, L Study, V Kitchen, D Living, R Foyer. The bell's letter is painted small and faded, like a worn brand, in the bottom corner of the same wall's outer face (Stardos Stencil). Neighbouring rooms hide it, so letters turn up as rooms slide away, and none show through doors, windows or the conservatory glass.
- E/click rings. The word is B-E-L-V-E-D-E-R-E, nine rings, with the one E bell rung four times. A wrong ring sounds a semitone flat and clears progress; a mistaken B counts as a fresh start. No tune, counter or time limit. Progress survives slides, falls and sleep, before or after assembly.
- On the ninth ring the bells replay the word by themselves, one strike every 0.6 s, each heard from its own room, so the sound travels through the house. Then the Foyer trophy case's glass door swings open. E/click on the open display presents the trophy (“Bell Trophy”, “For whom the bell tolls”) and it joins the item row. It has no effect on the finale.
- The trophy's gold cup is shaped like an upside-down bell. `tools/capture.sh bells A screenshots/bells` covers every bell, letter, the open case and the presentation.

## Assembled-house ending (independent of tokens and bells)
Assembling all eight rooms closes the room doors and unlocks the conservatory. Assembly awards no coins; drawers stay closed until tried. The player can still walk and look around inside the final room. After queued rewards finish, looking through the center-facing glass starts the reveal: the center slab retracts east beneath the neighboring room (2.2 s after a 0.55 s door-closing allowance), pauses 0.35 s, and a cream-framed, glass-roofed conservatory rises over 5.5 s from 6.2 m below room level. The opening is a real concrete-lined shaft: the meadow, island top, and walking collision leave the center clear, so the waiting glass roof and furnishings are visible below rather than hidden by a black plane. Its floor seats flush, then a 0.55 s settling pause ends before the aligned room doors open. The reveal waits for a clear view through the glass instead of taking over the camera; binocular and stopwatch views are unavailable during it. Pause freezes both retraction and lifting. The logical puzzle retains its solved board and center gap; the conservatory supplies the walkable floor and counts as indoors.

The conservatory has a layered broadleaf tree in a tapered terracotta planter, using the same branching, foliage mesh and material as the outdoor trees. Two wooden garden armchairs with sage cushions face a shared low tea table on a pale green rug in the southeast bay. A low planting bench occupies the northwest bay; ferns and broadleaf plants in terracotta and sage pots balance the other sides. There are no flowers or fruit. The red key rests on a paneled wooden pedestal with a pale stone top near the south entrance. The tree and pedestal remain the focal points, with a clear east aisle and open approaches from all four doors. E/click lifts the key into the same item-reward layout as the binoculars, with “Red Key” and “To your success”. After arriving, it waits for a fresh key, mouse or gamepad press before flying into the red inventory icon; early presses and key repeats cannot dismiss it. Pickup pauses with the menu and cannot be repeated. The key remains in inventory after use. The red front door has matching raised panels, brass handles, flush keyhole plates, red casings and house emblems on its interior and exterior faces. The keyhole beneath the handle signals that it needs a key; both emblems light together on unlocking. Interacting while carrying the key inserts, turns and withdraws it over 1.3 s, briefly holding player controls and pausing with the menu. Only then does unlocking light the house emblem, reveal the carpet and rainbow, and start the existing 3.5 s opening; passage stays blocked until fully open.

Crossing the doorway eases the camera 20 s along a red carpet under a pastel rainbow. Plain white Fondamento text without outlines: "Belvedere" (1.8 m high, fades in over the first 10 s); on arrival "A game by Nick Schaefer" (4 s fade), a 3 s wait, then "Thank you for playing" (4 s fade). The lower two lines sit closer to the camera so the three lines have equal visible gaps. One second after the thank-you is fully visible, run totals at bottom left and “Any key to continue” at bottom right fade in over 1.4 s. The ending then waits indefinitely for a fresh keyboard press before fading back to the main menu; held keys, mouse clicks/motion, and presses before the results finish appearing cannot dismiss it. Pause hides the results and freezes the sequence; Resume continues it. Once the results are ready, any fresh key (including Escape/P) continues instead of pausing. Stemless flowers sprout every 0.15 s, only inside the camera frustum, 2.5–12 m ahead, from a 160-instance MultiMesh without shadows.

Run tracking is local to the current playthrough and resets only for a new game. Shifts count every completed room move, including reversals, and accumulate across bed resets. Sleeps count confirmed bed sleeps plus fall recoveries once each; the total has a trailing asterisk if any were falls. Cancelling sleep and blocked shifts do not count. Time starts when play begins, pauses for every button menu (including settings, controls and sleep confirmation), and stops when the rainbow sequence begins. Slides, item inspection, sleep transitions, and falls remain part of active time. Results use “Shifts”, “Sleeps”, and “Time” with minutes:seconds (hours when needed); no tracking counters appear during ordinary play. `tools/try_conservatory.sh` starts a playable fixture one southward Foyer shift from assembly. `tools/capture.sh conservatory A screenshots/conservatory` verifies the final shift, reveal, physical key pickup, inventory layout, and door unlock. `tests/conservatory_tests.gd` covers view gating, walking, collision, pause, pickup, and entry into the original finale. `tests/run_stats_tests.gd` verifies accounting and menu timing; `tools/capture.sh ending A screenshots/ending --ending-only` captures the ending and results using staged totals.

## Study safe and fruit clues
- Four plain buttons count fresh taps independently and in any order: red 3, yellow 2, orange 4, green 1. Turning the handle checks the counts. A wrong attempt rattles and clears all taps; a correct one opens the door and presents the binoculars. No digits, tallies or labels. Safe state survives slides and falls.
- Clues: 3 apples on the Kitchen prep table, 2 separate bananas on the lower shelf of Dining's china cabinet, 4 oranges on Living's 0.90 m sideboard, 1 pear on the Bedroom dresser. Each clue renders only on pixels whose camera ray enters its own room through a window or a fully closed glass door. It is never visible indoors, through open or moving doors, or from above. No outline or shadow pass, which would leak it. Fruit stays out of static batches so its mask follows slides.

## Binoculars
B raises them along one arc, cuts through dark to a north-up orthographic overview of the live manor, and B or Escape returns to the exact first-person view; P still pauses. Escape also cancels raising and consumes repeated presses while lowering. Overview only: room-coloured roofs replace ceilings, carrying home-order numbers 1 2 3 / 4 · 5 / 6 7 8 that move with the rooms. Door lintels are hidden. Window-wall tops get black 0.34 m borders; the Foyer’s front-door edge gets a matching full-width red border, using the door’s red. Ordinary doorways get none. A small cream head-and-shoulders silhouette marks the occupied room in its southeast roof corner, clear of the number; in the gap or on the lawn it marks the player’s position. Shadows stay on. Movement, interaction and slides are locked while viewing. The player starts without them; opening the Study safe is the only way to earn them. The reward presents the binoculars on the left with large “Binoculars” lettering and “Press B to use” alongside, in Fondamento. The caption fades with the presentation and hides during pause.

## Stopwatch and foundation hatch
A fixed, shallow metal hatch sits in the northwest foundation square, initially beneath Dining. It becomes visible only when that square is empty, and is hidden with its interaction colliders disabled during slides. Its brass pull opens the hinged lid and automatically awards the watch. The watch is revealed inside as the lid opens, rests briefly, then lifts vertically out of the recess and arcs into its reward presentation; no second pickup interaction. Movement and interaction stay locked through opening and presentation. No key or additional code. The recess retains the solid walking floor. Once the watch clears the recess, the lid gently closes on its own with a quiet latch sound. The empty hatch is no longer interactive, and stays closed and empty when rooms cover and uncover it. Pause suspends both opening and closing. From the default opening, the first normally walkable access takes ten slides.

The original brass stopwatch has an ivory face and a centered number-only readout: the exact minimum room slides needed to assemble the house from the current board and player component. Walks cost zero; the lawn is equivalent to the sole gap. Deliberate fall/respawn shortcuts are excluded. The reverse 0–1 BFS builds incrementally within a small per-frame budget; the watch only displays completed distances, never provisional estimates. The count refreshes after committed slides and occupancy changes, reaches zero when assembled, and does not count tokens or elapsed time.

The reward uses the same fresh-press acceptance as other items, with “Stopwatch”, “Shows the fewest shifts needed for assembly.” and “Press T to use”. T raises/lowers the owned watch at the lower-right edge, lifting from below with a slight wrist-like turn but no visible hand. Escape also lowers it, including during the raise. P pauses it. Starting to walk automatically lowers the watch, with movement and looking available immediately during lowering; interactions and binocular use wait until it is stowed. Walking does not dismiss binoculars. Accepting the reward sends it into a small stopwatch icon beside the coin HUD and, when owned, binoculars. The inventory icon appears only after acquisition and carries no count; raise the watch to read its numerical hint. Pause hides and suspends its presentation/inspection; returning to title or falling clears inspection while preserving ownership. Controls lists Stopwatch — T only while owned.

`tools/try_stopwatch.sh` opens a playable ten-slide fixture on the lawn beside the unopened hatch, with one token in hand and the stopwatch still uncollected. `tools/capture.sh stopwatch A screenshots/stopwatch` verifies and captures discovery, collection, use and Controls. `tests/stopwatch_tests.gd` covers the real interaction rays, state retention, input lifecycle and exact counts.

## Exterior and fall
- A meadow with low hills. 15 near trees, eight mixed broadleaf/pine groves plus four lone trees, and a hazy 88–104 m treeline with a gap behind the rainbow. All are composed for views from the house. Keep the Bedroom window tree at (-17, -6.9), the current minimum clearance from the house, and an open south carpet approach.
- Walking off the meadow edge gives a ~15 s fall past a flat dark casing whose underside carries a large unlit QR code (https://puzzaholics.com/belvedere-assembly, error correction Q, `tools/gen_qr.sh`). The QR fills an exact opening in the casing, not a coplanar backing, which flickered. Then the screen fades and the player wakes near the foot of the bed in the Bedroom's current position with all progress kept.

## Title, pause, menus
- Sleeping: the Bedroom bed becomes interactable after the first completed room shift. E or left click opens “Start over?” with the smaller label “Sleep to reset the rooms. Keep your tokens and items.” and Sleep / Cancel, initially focusing Cancel. Cancel or a pause key leaves the layout untouched. Sleep fades out, restores the exact initial room arrangement (including random starts), and fades in beside the bed without returning to the title. Collected and spent tokens, opened drawers, bell progress, the trophy, owned items, and safe/hatch progress remain unchanged. The room-slide count resets; the bed waits for another shift before becoming active again. Falling still preserves the room arrangement. `tools/capture.sh sleep A screenshots/sleep` verifies the confirmation, transition, and retained inventory; `tests/bed_sleep_tests.gd` covers activation, cancellation, preservation, and reset geometry.
- The seed A title is painted on the Bedroom's west wall: large "Belvedere" and "Play" in ink, with no backdrop. Both fade over 0.55 s when Play captures the pointer; control returns at the same view. The 3D scene fades in over 1.4 s on launch while the menu stays visible.
- The permanent wall fixture is a spaced 3×3 grid of warm-gray squares, static and plain, riding with the Bedroom.
- Escape or P pauses (Controls shows "Esc / P"), and the same key resumes from the pause menu, not from Settings or Controls. Reward presentations never accept either key. Web exception: Escape does not resume, because browsers never grant pointer lock from Escape (it is not a user-activation key) and the menu would bounce back; P and Resume do.
- Web: losing pointer lock during play opens the pause menu, because browsers end the lock themselves (Escape, tab switches, clicks outside the canvas) without passing Escape to the game. Lock requests resolve after the click, so only a granted lock that ends, or a request still unmet after 1 s, pauses. During binocular or stopwatch use, losing the lock lowers the item instead; a subsequent click recaptures the pointer without opening pause. Slides and falls finish first, matching Escape. `tests/pointer_tests.gd` covers this with `--web-entry`.
- Period toggles a frame-rate readout ("60 FPS") in the top left corner, on every screen and across the Play reload but not between sessions. It is a diagnostic, so Controls doesn't list it. It takes the key before any "any key" handler, so it never accepts a reward or dismisses the ending.
- Title and pause have Play/Resume, Controls, Settings and Sound: On/Off. Controls lists Move, Look, Interact, Pull lever and Pause, plus Binoculars — B and Stopwatch — T only while owned. Settings: FOV 60–100, sensitivity, invert-Y, master/effects/music volume, persisted in `user://settings.cfg`.
- Buttons are plain lettering: hover/focus tints the word and pressing lowers it 2 px. Focus tints appear only after keyboard input, and hover never moves focus (Enter after hovering Quit must not quit).

## Audio
- Recorded CC0 foley shaped by `tools/gen_audio.sh`, kept at natural speed (pitch-stretching sounded synthetic). Three footstep sounds every 1.05 m: soft (wood rooms, rugs, carpet), normal (tile rooms, gap) and grass (quiet). Lever grab/return clicks and a blocked clunk. One 2 s slide sound that lands on docking; music ducks 6 dB. Only the nearest opening and nearest closing door are voiced. Locked-door rattle = handle catch plus one push. Coin acquisition and insertion clinks. The wall bells are one recorded small-bell strike at its natural length: `bell_clear`, and `bell_off`, the same recording a semitone flat and slightly darker. Neither is pitch-varied, so a ring's tone stays a reliable clue; the end-of-word peal reuses `bell_clear`. Reward drawers unlock with a recorded latch and a light knock, open with a quiet wooden menu tap when opening and the muted lever-stop sound when rattled. The conservatory reuses the room-slide rolling layer without timber creaks: the slab has a very soft, muffled 2.2 s slide, and the lift has a fuller 5.5 s rolling rise with a muted landing. Both have short soft tails, start with their respective movements, and pause with the menu. Pickup uses a soft mechanical click, and unlocking uses the existing latch. The foundation hatch reuses a quiet opening creak and closing latch, and the stopwatch leaving its recess uses a soft existing mechanical click; the watch does not tick.
- A distant bird loop plays only outdoors (gap or lawn), very quiet (−33 dB). Menu presses (never hover) play one brass latch click (`ManorAudio.MENU_CLICK`), deferred so muting is silent and unmuting is audible.
- Music: replaceable loop `assets/audio/manor_theme.ogg` (an original felt-piano loop) at 35%. It starts after 3 s and fades in over 5 s, multiplied by slide ducking and the fade under piano songs.
- Piano songs: `assets/audio/piano_song_0.ogg` to `piano_song_6.ogg`, one per token, 30–60 s, mono, quiet and not looped; repeated if fewer than seven. All are CC0 stand-ins until the composer's arrive; their source recordings stay out of git. `tools/gen_audio.py` matches loudness and ends longer pieces at a quiet moment with a 3 s fade. On web they decode on first play rather than at load.
- Web: the Effects and Music buses must exist in `default_bus_layout.tres`, because non-threaded web audio ignores buses added at runtime. Music fade follows the heard playback position because browsers hold audio until the first input. Headless runs skip playback but emit `ManorAudio.cued` for tests.

## Art and rendering
- Late-afternoon warm light, cream plaster, warm wood, one strong accent per room, chunky readable furniture and little clutter. Flat-shaded low poly, original geometry generated in code; no downloaded models or textures. No reference images are stored.
- Godot 4.7 Compatibility renderer, so web matches desktop. One shared toon shader (`shaders/toon.gdshader`): 3-band ramp and a faint paper grain. Inverted-hull warm-brown ink outlines, never black. The grain, outline wobble, planks and tiles are sampled in mesh-local space, because world-space sampling made surfaces swim during slides. No post-processing, bloom or fog.
- Exterior: only window walls and the red front-door wall—the exterior faces of the finished puzzle—have warm-grey lap siding over a 0.3 m charcoal base on their outward face (mesh −Z; interior trims sit on +Z). This finish stays with each wall as rooms shift; ordinary connecting-door walls retain their plain room colour, including when exposed to the lawn, gap or a neighbour's window. The toon shader supplies one siding colour for the whole house with no added geometry. Courses are 0.18 m with a lap shadow line. They use world height, which slides never change, so they line up across wall pieces and rooms. Lines widen to at least a pixel and lighten to match, so distant walls don't alias. Sided east/west walls' outer end faces are house corners and get siding (`siding_corners`). Other end and reveal faces on sided walls (door jambs, window reveals) are cream casings. `tools/capture.sh exterior` checks facades, the gap, corners and interior windows.
- Sun `#FFD9A8`, pitch −28°, yaw 235°, 2 PSSM splits, 40 m shadows; sun patches on floors are a key image. Wall corners are butt-jointed and trims sit on their owner's interior side: no coplanar opaque faces with different colours (tested). No hull expansion on adjoining wall planes or thin door joinery, which caused detached ink strips.

## Performance
Most cost is the sun's shadow pass. Batched outline shells are separate non-casting geometry. Furniture the fixed sun can never reach through its own room's openings casts no shadow (`scripts/manor_sun_reach.gd`, ray-checked by `tests/shadow_tests.gd`). Repeated opaque pieces are batched per rigid assembly and room shell; glass stays separate. Foliage is unshaded vertex colour, merged into one mesh per 32 m cell or treeline sector; only trees within 46 m cast shadows. Because unshaded foliage can't darken, shrubs and casting grove trees sit outside tree and house shadows. MultiMesh batches keep custom bounds. Godot's dummy renderer doesn't store MultiMesh transforms, so headless geometry checks keep their own. First-use work would stall the only thread on web, so it happens before the scene appears: `ManorAudio` decodes every stream except the piano songs once during web load (kept across the Play reload), and on web and Windows `scripts/manor_shader_warmup.gd` draws the title with the HUD's 2D variants, an interior, the exterior, the overview and the finale for one frame each behind the opaque fade, with the menu hidden. Anything hidden at startup should reuse a compiled shader: the coin's engraving is a toon `print_cutout`, because its own shaded `StandardMaterial3D` froze a slow PC for seconds on the first insert. The Play reload recompiles a few variants on its first frame, also behind the fade. Untried next steps: per-material merging of room singletons, and portal culling. Measure with `tools/profile.sh`.

## Rejected — don't reintroduce
Camera shake (and its setting); pulsing, scaling or animated room indicators; floor targets, arrows and check marks; a target diagram in the overview; completion chimes; outdoor wind, per-room ambient loops and a front-door creak; a separate rug footstep; tonal or bright hard-floor steps; birds indoors; digits or tally marks on the safe; menu hover pills, a circular Play icon and ornamental diamonds; a smiley meadow underside; billboard or pancake trees and evenly scattered trees; ordinary doorway borders in the overview; lit decorative lamps; per-room exterior siding tints.
